## The question

How might a digital experience help students find timely peer support and self-directed mental wellbeing resources without making the experience feel clinical or overwhelming?

SereniTea was an academic prototype, not a clinical service. The team used it to practice user research, interaction design, prototyping, and usability testing.

## My contribution

I helped the team frame the problem, work through the research and prototype, and communicate the final concept. In the final presentation, I introduced the problem and project context, then helped walk through the product demonstration.

## Research

The team:

- Interviewed five prospective users about how they seek support and what makes an online experience feel trustworthy.
- Reviewed comparable experiences, including 7 Cups and Headspace.
- Turned the research into prioritized requirements and early flows.
- Built low-fidelity and high-fidelity prototypes.
- Ran a five-person usability study and synthesized the recurring points of friction.

## What we learned

Three issues mattered most during testing:

1. **Errors needed to be visible.** Participants were less confident when the interface failed quietly or did not explain what to do next.
2. **Support matching needed to feel trustworthy.** People wanted clearer expectations about who they might meet and how a match was chosen.
3. **Self-help navigation needed a simpler path.** Too many options made it harder to decide where to begin.

These findings shifted the work from adding more features to clarifying feedback, expectations, and navigation.

## Design response

The high-fidelity prototype organized the concept around two routes:

- Peer support for someone who wants to talk with another person.
- Self-directed resources for someone who would rather explore privately.

The interface used a calm visual language, clearer status feedback, and more direct labels so the next action was easier to understand.

## What I would do next

A production version would need much more than a polished prototype. The next round should include accessibility testing, safety and escalation policies, moderation design, privacy review, and evaluation with qualified mental health professionals. Support matching would also need transparent criteria and careful abuse prevention.

## Privacy and evidence

Raw interview notes, participant identities, student IDs, and private team materials are intentionally not published. Public visuals should only be added after teammate permission and a privacy review.

## Takeaway

This project taught me that a friendly interface is not enough. Trust comes from clear feedback, understandable choices, honest limits, and careful testing with the people a product is meant to support.
